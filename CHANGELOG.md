### Added

- `examples/dpa-review.md` and `.json`, the Art. 28(3) GDPR clause review of the two bundled synthetic agreements, generated by `make demo`, checked by `make check-generated`, listed in the verification manifest and linked from the Pages site card.
- DPA review: a ninth, non-blocking row for Art. 28 Abs. 3 UAbs. 2 DSGVO (the processor immediately informs the controller of an instruction that infringes data protection law). Absent, it yields `review`, not `missing`.
- Guidance from legal on the intake page (F9a, [ADR 0012](docs/adr/0012-intake-guidance.md)). `legal-function-os intake-page --guidance PATH` reads a team's own guidance file (`legal-function-os.intake-guidance.v1`): short texts per request type, each with the four keys of a `portal.share.v1` resource, who prepared it, and a review record (`approval`: who reviewed it for requesters, the date, and the SHA-256 of the type, title, passage and source as reviewed). None is bundled, so the default page, `make demo` and the Pages copy show none. The chosen type's section opens with the guidance as static text, under "Guidance from legal for this kind of request" and a line saying it is general information, not legal advice, and optional; each text shows its source and "Legal's guidance file records that NAME reviewed this text for requesters on DATE." It has no link or control, and no word the page adds says approved. Every problem stops the build with its place, and nothing is written: no review record, a text that reads empty on the page (blank, or only invisible characters), a digest that no longer matches the text (the message names the digest it has now), a review recorded by the person who prepared the text (compared ignoring case, spacing and invisible characters), a date that is not one, a link or unpaired surrogate, a passage over 800 characters, more than three texts for a type, a repeated id, look-alike titles in a type, or an unknown key; a knowledge-portal document is refused. A text with no type, or a type the policy lacks, is a note. Guidance stays out of the page script and its data block: a browser test saves the same answers from pages with and without guidance and gets byte-identical files. A review record is not authentication; the digest only keeps a text changed after its review off the page. `examples/intake-page.html` changes only in its style block and the style hash of its Content-Security-Policy. The question sheet lists each type's guidance, and `tests/test_intake_guidance.py` covers the checks, the page and the command.
- [ADR 0011](docs/adr/0011-requester-vocabulary.md): the requester-stage vocabulary (F3). The pilot's six stage ids (`draft`, `received`, `in_review`, `approved`, `ready`, `closed`) are the repository's requester-stage vocabulary, additive only, and every pilot state maps to one of them; `declined` and `duplicate` map to `closed`. The core shared space keeps its `legal-function-os.shared-space.v1` words unchanged: its `in_review` is the pilot's stage, `blocked` stays a core flag, not a stage, and its two German next-step sentences are v1 values. No German stage labels are added: German comes back only through a new ADR, with a named German-speaking reviewer, a chosen form of address and wording a team can replace. `tests/test_requester_vocabulary.py` pins the ids and their order, the stage of every pilot state and the core's words. No code or output changes.
- Pilot triage verbs (F10, [ADR 0010](docs/adr/0010-triage-verbs.md)). In triage, an onboarded matter owner can decline a matter (`decline`), close it as a duplicate of another matter they can see (`mark_duplicate`), or snooze it (`snooze`), and each verb claims the matter as `complete_triage` does. So a matter owner can now close a matter that cannot leave triage, such as one outside the pilot charter; before, only the requester could withdraw it. `declined` and `duplicate` are new final states that accept no further command, map to `rejected` in `legal-workflow-controls.v1` (additive; `rejected` takes precedence over `blocked`), and are kept in the decision record, bound to the version they closed. Each needs a reason of at least 20 visible characters (invisible characters, such as a zero-width space or a Hangul filler, do not count; the reason is kept as typed, with runs of whitespace collapsed) that a person types and the requester reads; the room proposes none, and the software never declines, marks a duplicate or snoozes on its own. A duplicate's original must be visible to the owner and not itself a duplicate, an unknown and a hidden matter get the same refusal, and the requester sees the original only if they asked for it too. A snooze is a recorded pause, not a state: a reason and either a time with a time zone, at most 30 days ahead, or a question for the requester, who answers it in the room (`answer_snooze`; the question and the answer each need at least 10 visible characters); the owner can end it (`end_snooze`). A triage verb or a withdrawal also ends it, and its time passing ends it without writing anything: `supervised-agent/src/pilot/snooze.py` works it out from the events on the matter's one clock for the room, the requester view, the parent ledger and the metrics. While it runs, the parent ledger and the pilot metrics count it as waiting on the requester; on a request legal has not acknowledged it is legal's first response. A declined or duplicate matter has no resolution target, as for a withdrawal. The requester sees "Closed: declined by legal" or "Closed: duplicate of another matter" with the reason (for a duplicate, "Legal closed this request as a duplicate of another matter", whatever state the original is in), or, during a snooze, the reason and the question. The runbook, charter, identity controls and case study say so, and `supervised-agent/tests/test_pilot_triage_verbs.py` and a browser test cover it.
- A shared print block, `PRINT_CSS`, next to the design tokens in the core and in the agent's copy ([ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-design-tokens.md), amended). It restates the light tokens inside `@media print`, so a page read in dark mode still prints dark ink on a light page; it is derived from the light tokens and tested against them. - Message drafts (`legal-function-os message-drafts`, owner decision 8, [ADR 0009](docs/adr/0009-message-drafts.md)): a local, inbound-only adapter that turns a synthetic message export (JSON lines, one message per line, every sender a `syn-` handle) into `legal-request.v1` drafts for legal to complete. Values are copied only from labelled lines such as `Value band: <50k`, matched like CSV headers and copied as written; nothing is read from the rest of the text, the subject is the title and the whole text the description. Every field the intake page always asks is in every draft, and one a message does not state is left blank (`""`) and named. Every blank but `type` is an error, so `validate` and `triage` refuse the draft until legal fills it instead of deciding it LOW on defaults; a blank `type` is only a warning, as a missing one is, and `triage` then decides the draft without the type's own rules, so the drafts say to fill it before triage. Drafts are `REQ-DRAFT-1` and so on, with the message id in `x_message_id`. The `syn-` check marks synthetic data; senders are not verified. It reads one file, connects to no chat or mail service, sends and decides nothing, and is a CLI command, not an MCP tool. `make demo` writes `examples/message-drafts.md` and `.json` from the bundled export, `src/legal_function_os/data/sample_messages.jsonl`, which the wheel now ships; both are in the verification manifest and `make package-check` runs the command from the installed wheel. A test checks that `docs/CLI.md` lists every command.
- [ADR 0008](docs/adr/0008-pilot-local-hardening.md): authentication is not built (owner decision 1). Sign-in needs the organisation's identity provider and network access, so controls 1 to 11 in `supervised-agent/docs/pilot/IDENTITY_CONTROLS.md` stay preconditions for practitioner use, and the pilot server gets local hardening instead (see Changed), which is not authentication. New tests show that a final approval is refused when the approver's tier is too low, when the approver is also the requester or matter owner (no command can make the owner the approver, so that test writes the assignment directly into the store), and when the approver decided a change, and that no HTTP route registers an actor or changes a role or tier. A browser test shows that the review room still renders its package frame and that a page on another origin cannot frame it.

Tests check that `legal-function-os-mcp` starts over stdio only, refuses transport, host and port options and keeps no module-level server object, and that every plugin MCP server is a local command that starts it.
